National Projects

27 permanent nation-wide investments. They are expensive, they cannot be sold, and each one adds +20 score — which pushes you up into a harder war range whether you wanted that or not.

How projects work

Every project is a coefficient on a formula that already exists, never a new system. That design rule is what lets a game support 27+ projects without the codebase collapsing under its own weight. If a project would need its own table, it gets redesigned instead.

  • Permanent. Once built, a project cannot be sold, demolished or rebuilt.
  • Nation-wide. Effects apply across every city, not per city.
  • +20 score each. Ten projects is 200 score you did not choose to add to your war range.
Projects narrow you

Because they are permanent and score-bearing, each project makes the nation you are becoming a little more fixed. Building the whole military set turns you into a target for anyone hunting military score; building the whole economic set makes you rich and reachable. There is no reset.

The stacking-order trap

The three urban planning projects give flat discounts on new city cost, and they stack: $50,000,000, $100,000,000 and $150,000,000 come off the price directly.

The Manifest Destiny policy is a multiplier. Order of application changes the final number materially:

Why order matters — city 30, all three projects, Manifest Destiny
Base cost$1,102,025,000
Flat first, then multiplier (correct)$761,923,750
Multiplier first, then flat (wrong)$746,923,750
Difference$15,000,000

The engine applies flat discounts first, then multipliers, and there is a test that fails if that order is ever changed.
