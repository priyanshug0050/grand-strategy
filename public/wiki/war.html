War, Combat and Espionage

Wars in SOVRA run over days, not minutes. You cannot be knocked out in one hit, you cannot be attacked by someone overwhelmingly larger, and every battle roll is stored so any fight can be replayed and audited afterwards.

Score

Score is both a power rating and a matchmaking weight. That dual role is the most important thing to understand about it.

score = 10 + (cityCount - 1) * 100 + totalInfrastructure / 40 + projects * 20 + militaryScore

Ships are a trap

A ship is worth 1 score — 40× a tank. Building a navy pushes you up into the war range of much larger nations without giving you a proportionate ability to fight them. Soldiers are the most score-efficient unit in the game by a wide margin.

War range

You can declare on anyone from -25% below to 75% above your score.

A nation at score 1,000
Can attack750 — 1,750
Can be attacked by571.4 — 1,333.3

The range is asymmetric on purpose: you can always be hit by someone meaningfully bigger, never by someone overwhelmingly bigger. It is the anti-griefing backbone of the whole game, and it is the reason score management is a real strategic decision rather than a number that only goes up.

You hold 5 offensive war slots (6 with the Pirate Economy project) and can be attacked in at most 3 at once.

Military Action Points

Attacks cost MAP. You regain 1 per turn up to a maximum of 12, so a full bar takes 12 turns — 1.0 days.

This is the mechanism that makes wars take days. You cannot save up an infinite reserve and delete someone in an afternoon, and you cannot be deleted while you sleep.

The three-roll system

Every battle is decided by 3 independent rolls. Each side rolls a random fraction of its army value, between 40% and 100%, and the higher roll wins that exchange.

Rolls wonResult
0Utter Failure — no damage at all
1Pyrrhic Victory
2Moderate Success
3Immense Triumph — full damage, control state granted

Damage scales with the tier: an Immense Triumph deals full damage, a Pyrrhic Victory deals a third of it, an Utter Failure deals none.

Why the band is 40–100%

This is the master tuning knob of the entire game. Widen it and combat becomes coin-flippy; narrow it and combat is pure arithmetic with no reason to ever fight an even match. At 40–100%, a 2.5× advantage is a near-certain sweep, but anything below that stays genuinely uncertain — and an underdog can still win.

Every battle is reproducible

The random seed for each battle is stored. Any fight can be replayed and will produce the identical result, down to the casualty counts. If you think a result was wrong, it can be checked rather than argued about.

Damage and its cap

damage = (attackerValue - defenderValue * 0.5) * coefficient * jitter(0.85–1.05) * (rollsWon / 3)
cap = cityInfrastructure * 0.5 + 100Per battle, per city. No city can be destroyed in a single hit — this is what makes wars multi-day affairs.

The attack always targets your highest-infrastructure city. Airstrikes aimed at anything other than infrastructure deal 33% collateral damage to it. On an Immense Triumph there is a 10% chance per battle of destroying an improvement outright.

The supply rule

The most important rule in SOVRA combat

An unsupplied unit contributes zero army value but still takes casualties. One clause makes logistics mandatory without needing a separate logistics system.

Tanks need munitions and gasoline. Aircraft and ships need more of both. Soldiers fight unarmed at reduced value, so an army with no munitions is not helpless — but it is a fraction of its paper strength.

Supply is allocated to vehicles before soldiers, and that priority is derived rather than assumed: a tank converts each munition into more army value than a soldier does, so a limited stockpile is worth more in the tanks. The engine checks this rather than hard-coding it.

armyValue = unarmedSoldiers * 1 + armedSoldiers * 1.75 + tanks * 40 + defenderPopulation / 400 (defender only)

Fortifying

Digging in costs 3 action points and raises the casualties an attacker takes against you by 25%. It ends the moment you attack, so it cannot be stacked with offence — a turn is a real choice between hitting back and holding on.

It costs the same as a ground battle deliberately. A free defensive action taken every single turn is not a decision, and a defender with no decisions is just a target.

The comeback clause

Only an Immense Triumph grants you a control state — but any victory nullifies your enemy's control over you. A losing player always has a cheap, achievable goal: win one roll and break their grip. Without this asymmetry, losing the first battle would mean losing the war.

Loot

loot never exceeds 75% of their money loot can never take a nation below $1,000,000

Both limits matter. The cap stops a single defeat from ending someone's game; the floor guarantees a beaten nation still has enough to rebuild.

Losing a war

  • 10% of money and 10% of resources taken
  • Up to 4% infrastructure lost in every city
  • 2 days of beige, stacking per war lost
  • Credits are not lootable; the alliance bank is

Beige makes you immune to new declarations — existing wars continue. New nations start with 14 days of it. Leaving beige is one-way: you can never go back voluntarily.

Alliances are not playable yet

The engine models alliance banks, alliance tax and colour trade blocs, and this page documents those rules because they are real code. But there is no interface to found or join an alliance yet — it is the next major system being built. Until then, the alliance-bank line above describes a rule with nothing to apply it to.
