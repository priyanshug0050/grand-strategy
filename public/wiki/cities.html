Cities, Infrastructure and Land

Cities are where everything in SOVRA physically happens. Three separate cost curves govern them, and each one bends in a different place — which is why the cheapest thing to buy changes as your nation grows.

Improvement slots

Every building you own occupies a slot, and slots come from infrastructure alone. One slot per 50 infrastructure, rounded down.

slots = floor(infrastructure / 50)

A new city starts with 10 infrastructure and 250 land, which means 0 slots. Your first purchase in any new city is always infrastructure, because until you have it there is nowhere to put anything.

Slots are the real constraint

Money is renewable; slots are not. Past about 1,000 infrastructure the interesting question stops being "can I afford this building" and becomes "what am I giving up to fit it". Hospitals, police stations and power plants all compete for the same space as the buildings that actually earn.

Infrastructure cost

Infrastructure is bought in blocks of 100, and the unit price climbs with how much you already have.

unitCost = 300 + ((currentInfra - 10) ^ 2.2) / 710The exponent is what makes tall cities expensive and wide nations viable.
The offset quirk

Because of the - 10 term, infrastructure is not cheapest at zero — the minimum unit price sits at exactly 10 infrastructure, which is where cities start. This is inherited from Politics & War and it is not a bug.

Founding a new city

New cities are the game's main long-term money sink, and the price is cubic in how many you already have.

cost = 50,000 * (X - 1)^3 + 150,000 * X + 75,000 where X = your current city count

Discount order matters

Project discounts (Urban Planning and friends) are flat subtractions and they stack. The Manifest Destiny policy is a multiplier. The engine applies the flat discounts first, then the multiplier — applying them the other way round produces a materially different price, and it is a classic source of bugs in this genre. Cost never falls below $1.

Why buildings cost steel

Commerce, civil and military buildings cost refined materials as well as money. Raw extraction and manufacturing buildings cost money only.

That asymmetry is deliberate. If a steel mill required steel, a new player with no industry could never start one — a chicken-and-egg trap with no exit. And if nothing consumed refined goods domestically, manufacturing would exist purely to sell on the market, which makes a rich nation able to skip industry entirely and simply buy its way to maximum commerce.

As it stands, growth costs production time rather than only cash, and steel and aluminium have a use even when the market price is bad.

Barracks are deliberately free of materials

Soldiers are the one military unit a nation with no industry can still raise. A player who has been bombed flat needs a route back to defending themselves, and $3,000 with no material cost is that route.

Demolishing returns 50% of the materials and no money. A full refund would let you launder value by cycling build and demolish; a zero refund would make one misclick on a 300-steel drydock unrecoverable.
