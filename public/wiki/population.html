Population, Disease and Crime

Population is not a resource you store — it is recomputed from your city every turn. Nothing is remembered, so nothing can silently drift. Understand the density term and you understand most of SOVRA's city planning.

Base population

Infrastructure creates people. Age adds a slow drift on top.

base = (infra * 100) + (infra / 1,000) * (100 * ageDays / 3)
Live from the engine
1,000 infra, day 0100,000
1,000 infra, day 365112,167
2,000 infra, day 365224,333

This is the number every other term in this page reduces. Disease and crime kill a fraction of the base, and the age multiplier scales what survives.

Density and disease

Density is infrastructure per unit of land, scaled by 100.

density = (infra * 100) / land
diseaseRate% = (0.01 * density^2 - 25) / 100 * 100 + infra / 1,000 - hospitals * 2.5 + pollution * 0.05Clamped to 0–100%. Deaths = rate × infra × 100.

The squared density term is the most load-bearing line in the game. It is what makes land worth buying, what forces hospitals into the slot competition, and what turns pollution into a real cost rather than a cosmetic number.

Density ignores live population — deliberately

Disease is computed from infrastructure and land, never from the current population. If it fed back on live population you would get an oscillation: people die, density falls, disease falls, people return, density rises. The city would never settle. Because the inputs are static, evaluating the same city twice always gives the same answer.

The disease floor

As density approaches zero the squared term vanishes, leaving a floor that land can never get below:

floor% = -25 + infra / 1,000 - hospitals * 2.5 + pollution * 0.05

The infrastructure term is the reason. Past roughly 250 infrastructure, no amount of land reaches 0% disease. That is not a bug — it is the mechanism that forces hospitals into a large city's improvement slots.

Each hospital removes a flat 2.5%, and the Clinical Research Center project removes another 2.5% on top.

Crime

Crime is the mirror of disease: it rises with population and falls with commerce and police stations.

crime% = ((103 - commerce)^2 + basePop * 0.1) / 111,111 - police * 2.5Crime deaths are weighted ×4 — a percent of crime hurts far more than a percent of disease.

The squared commerce term makes commerce buildings do double duty: they raise income and suppress crime, so a developed city is safer as well as richer. That is a better incentive than a flat penalty, and it means the answer to a crime problem is often "build a bank", not "build a police station".

The crime equation is our own — Politics & War has never published theirs. The shape is a large improvement on a linear version, which made large cities lose an absurd fraction of their people every turn, but the coefficients are still being tuned.

City age

Old cities are worth more. The multiplier is logarithmic, so it is negligible early and becomes a real retention reward later.

multiplier = 1 + ln(ageDays) / 15

Because it is logarithmic rather than linear, an old city can never run away with the game — but abandoning a mature city to found a fresh one always costs you something real.

A dead city floors, it does not vanish

Population can never fall below 10. Even at 100% disease the city survives as a shell you can rebuild from, rather than disappearing and taking its improvements with it.

How it all assembles

base = basePopulation(infra, ageDays) survivors = base - diseaseDeaths - (crimeDeaths * 4) population = max(survivors * ageMultiplier, 10)

Order matters: the age multiplier applies to survivors, not to the base. A city that is losing people to density loses the age bonus on those people too, which is why a badly shaped old city underperforms a well-shaped young one far more than the raw numbers suggest.

Every one of these figures is shown, expanded, on your population page in game. You never have to trust this wiki over what the game tells you — they are generated from the same code.
