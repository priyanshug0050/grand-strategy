Economy, Income and Production

Money comes from people, and people come from cities. Everything else in the economy is a chain: raw resources feed refining, refining feeds construction, and power sits underneath all of it as a hard on/off switch.

Income

Income is paid on the daily rollover, not every turn. It is per-capita and scales with your commerce rate.

incomePerCapitaPerDay = ((commerce / 50) * 0.725) + 0.725 income = incomePerCapitaPerDay * population
CommercePer capita / dayIncome at 100k people
0$0.725$72,500
25$1.088$108,750
50$1.450$145,000
75$1.813$181,250
100$2.175$217,500

Note the shape: at commerce 50 you earn exactly double the base rate. Commerce caps at 100%, or 115% with the International Trade Center project.

The 1000× trap

Politics & War documents a "Minimum Wage" of 725. That figure is for display only — the real per-capita constant in the income formula is 0.725. The thousandfold difference comes from the tax rate cancelling out. Anyone reimplementing these formulas from the wiki hits this, and the resulting nation earns a thousand times too much.

Power

Power is not a modifier. It is a threshold, and it is the most common reason a new player's city earns nothing.

powered = totalInfraCapacity >= cityInfrastructure fuelBurnedPerTurn = 0.1 per 100 infrastructure covered
PlantFuelInfra coveredCostUpkeepPollution
Coal PowerCoal500$5,000$1,200/day8
Oil PowerOil500$7,000$1,800/day6
Nuclear PowerUranium2,000$500,000$10,500/day0
Wind Powernone250$30,000$500/day0
501 infrastructure, one coal plant, nothing works

A coal plant covers exactly 500 infrastructure. At 500 you are fine. At 501 the city is unpowered and every manufacturing, civil and commerce building in it produces nothing — while still charging full upkeep. This is the single most expensive mistake available to a new player.

A plant with no fuel does not partially work. It produces no power and burns no fuel, and everything downstream of it goes idle. Mixed plant types are worst: if one type runs dry the whole city blacks out. Wind power needs no fuel at all, which is why it is worth its price in a nation that cannot reliably supply coal.

Your ledger names which of the two problems you have — not enough capacity, or no fuel for the capacity you built.

Raw extraction and refining

Raw extraction works without power. Refining does not.

ResourceBuildingInputs / dayOutput / dayRatio

AluminumAluminum Refinery3 bauxite62.00×
GasolineOil Refinery3 oil62.00×
MunitionsMunitions Factory3 lead62.00×

Manufacturing is far more profitable than extraction, and that gap is what creates the economy's tier structure: raw exporters at the bottom, refiners in the middle, consumers at the top. A nation that only mines is choosing to be someone else's supplier.

Farms are different

Farm output scales with land, not a flat rate.

foodPerTurn = land / 250 = land / 185 (with Mass Irrigation)

Antarctica produces 50% less food than everywhere else. Choose it only if you intend to import.

Nothing is produced from nothing

A refinery with no inputs produces zero and consumes zero — it never drives a stockpile negative. Mines can feed refineries in the same turn, so a self-sufficient chain works, but only if the mines actually produce enough that turn.

Stacking bonuses

Building several of the same improvement in one city pays a specialisation bonus.

CountManufacturingRaw extraction
1+0%+0%
2+12.5%+5.6%
3+25%+11.1%
4+37.5%+16.7%
5+50%+22.2%

Manufacturing gains 12.5% per building past the first, capping at 50%. Raw extraction reaches 50% at its build limit.

This is how SOVRA gets distinct nation archetypes without any explicit class system. Spreading one of everything across a city is always worse than committing to something.

What drains the treasury

Military upkeep

Paid daily, and it rises when you are at war.

Upkeep is a real constraint

If you cannot pay, units desert. The engine does not let you run a negative balance and quietly carry on — an army you cannot afford is an army you do not keep. Money floors at zero and the desertion is proportional.

Food

civilians: 1,000 people eat 1 food per turn soldiers: 1 / 750 per soldier at peace, 1 / 500 at war

Running out of food costs you 33% of gross income. That single penalty is what makes food strategic rather than an afterthought, and it is why a war fought without a food supply collapses on its own.

Improvement upkeep

Every building charges upkeep every day whether it produces anything or not. An unpowered city is not merely idle — it is actively losing money.

The derivation ledger

Everything above is visible in game, expanded. The economy page does not show you one income figure — it shows you which building earned what, which resource runs out in how many turns at your current burn rate, and, for anything producing nothing, why.

  • Per-building attribution — every line traces to the improvement that caused it
  • Resource runway — turns remaining per resource, not just a rate
  • Idle diagnosis — missing input, no power, or policy penalty, named explicitly
  • Material costs — what a building will consume, before you commit

This is the main thing SOVRA does differently. Politics & War hides the arithmetic and lets the community reverse-engineer it into spreadsheets that are half wrong. We would rather just show you.
